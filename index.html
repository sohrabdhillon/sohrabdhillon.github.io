MSE294 / Engineering Manufacturing & Design

Composite beam design, FEA, and modular coupling

Simulation Lead · Dec 2025–Apr 2026

A / Action

Evaluated four composite concepts using CAD, bending calculations, second-moment-of-area analysis, and FEA; then designed 1 mm slot-and-rod ONYX interfaces and refined laser-cut geometry for assembly.

D / Description

The work progressed from an individual MDF–XPS–CFRP sandwich beam to a four-beam system using corrugated balsa–CFRP members. The team design targeted a 0.5 kg midpoint load over a 280 mm span for 30 s, a ≤$7 material budget, and ≤9 g of connectors.

R / Result

The paired long-axis layout reduced simulated centre displacement by about 70% and centre stress by about 16% versus the initial orientation. ONYX connectors added 1.12 g and the projected material cost was $3.74. Physical testing later exposed lateral stability rather than visible material fracture as the dominant failure mode.

Engineering takeaway. Simulation supported the material and bending design, but the physical test showed that load geometry, overhang, connector consistency, and lateral stability governed the system response. Redesign recommendations included reducing overhang and adding central support/bracing.

Engineering Strategies & Practice II · Jan–Apr 2025

Classroom power distribution system

Concept development & requirements

A / Action

Translated client concerns into measurable engineering requirements, generated 70+ concepts, and narrowed them through feasibility screening, multivoting, graphical analysis, and a Pugh comparison before developing CAD layouts.

D / Description

The selected concept integrates four desk power stations with 16 additional receptacles, USB access, pop-up protection, and routed floor/wall cabling to reduce congestion around the classroom’s existing outlets.

R / Result

The Integrated Desk Power Station became the proposed conceptual design. A verification plan defined checks for coverage, charging capacity, usability, and maintenance; the project remained conceptual and no installed-system performance is claimed.

Engineering Strategies & Practice I · Sep–Dec 2024

Sustainable campus food-service system

System concept & stakeholder analysis

A / Action

Converted site research and stakeholder needs into accessibility, sustainability, food-safety, and service requirements; screened 52 proposals with the team and compared finalists using a Pugh chart and impact–effort analysis.

D / Description

The proposed system uses semi-autonomous delivery robots to collect food from nearby businesses and deliver it to an accessible Bahen Courtyard hub, with renewable-energy charging, allergen information, and a defined service boundary.

R / Result

The robot-and-hub concept was selected over vending-machine and food-court alternatives. The specification incorporated a 60% waste-diversion target and accessibility clearances for mobility-device users; the project was conceptual rather than a deployed service.
